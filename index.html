Research

Low-Cost AI/ML Water Quality Monitoring

Rutgers RAD Collaboratory (May—Jul 2026) → School of Environmental and Biological Sciences, under Dr. Siddhartha Roy (Aug 2026—Present) Role: Undergraduate Researcher

Overview

Spectrophotometers are a widely-used tool for detecting contaminants in water (such as chlorine) but they're expensive and impractical for field use, especially for rural communities who could benefit from water quality testing. Too little chlorine in water can leave it unsafe due to a lack of protection againt bacteria, while too much poses health risks to people who consume it. My research explores whether computer vision and machine learning can replace them: training models to read contaminant concentrations directly from photos, cheaply and accurately enough to work outside a lab. I joined this project in May 2026 through Rutgers' Chancellor's Signature Initiatives Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship, a competitive program pairing undergrads with faculty-led research. When the fellowship wrapped in July, my PI gave me the opportunity to stay on, and I've continued the work since. The work is now conducted within School of Environmental and Biological Sciences rather than New Brunswick Office for Research, where the fellowship was hosted.

What I've Built

  • Trained and iterated on CV/ML models that estimate contaminant concentration from images, aiming to match spectrophotometer-level accuracy at a fraction of the cost.
  • Generated custom training datasets from lab experiments, controlling for lighting, angle, and sample conditions to keep the data usable for real-world deployment, not just controlled settings.
  • Optimized image-based quantification algorithms in Python/scikit-learn, testing preprocessing and feature-extraction approaches to improve model reliability across varied lighting and camera conditions.

Current Focus

  • Improving model accuracy through iterative training/testing across different dataset compositions.
  • Reading and applying findings from published literature to refine our approach.
  • Building and hardening the mobile app for real field deployment. I'm working on moving it from a research prototype toward something usable outside the lab.
